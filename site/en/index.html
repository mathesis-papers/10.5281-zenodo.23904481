The 2n−1 arc minimum in Koljančić–Oblak, Problem 1.8 (p. 759)

Haobo Ma, Wenlin Zhang

Paper

How few directed connections can guarantee that a matrix has a particular rigidity property, regardless of the nonzero weights placed on those connections? Haobo Ma and Wenlin Zhang answer: twice the number of vertices minus one. Their argument combines a lower bound with a matching construction credited to Koljančić and Oblak.

The prescribed nonzero positions of a matrix form its support. Regard each position in row i, column j as an arc from vertex i to vertex j; loops count once, and parallel arcs are excluded. Every prescribed entry must be nonzero, with arbitrary real sign and magnitude. The rigidity condition, called the nonsymmetric strong spectral property or nSSP, has this equivalent test: if F commutes with A, meaning AF = FA, and F’s transposed positions vanish wherever A is nonzero, then F must be zero. No separate diagonal or trace condition is imposed.

A support “requires” nSSP if every permitted weighting has it, but “allows” nSSP if at least one does. For example, the diagonal matrix with entries 1 and 2 has nSSP: commutation kills F’s off-diagonal entries, and the support condition kills its diagonal entries. Replacing both weights by 1 permits a nonzero off-diagonal commuting F. Thus two isolated loops allow but do not require nSSP. The authors settle Koljančić–Oblak’s requiring-minimum question: for every positive integer n, the answer is 2n−1 arcs. Using the equivalence identified in that earlier work, this also answers Arav and collaborators’ corresponding question about patterns requiring the similarity-transversality property.

The lower bound connects commuting matrices with balanced flows on the graph. The space of matrices commuting with any n-by-n real matrix has dimension at least n. The authors explain this through a decomposition into cyclic blocks: polynomial actions within each block supply independent commuting maps whose total dimension is n. For a commuting F, assign the real number aᵢⱼfⱼᵢ to arc i to j. Commutation says that total incoming and outgoing quantities agree at every vertex. These flows may be negative, and loops automatically balance. Crucially, nSSP makes this assignment injective: if every assigned quantity vanishes, F must vanish too.

With m arcs and c connected components after directions are ignored, including isolated vertices, the balanced-flow space has dimension m−n+c. Comparing dimensions gives m ≥ 2n−c for any individual matrix with nSSP. A connected support therefore needs at least 2n−1 arcs even merely to allow nSSP. The authors then prove that requiring supports must be connected. Each component needs a loop; otherwise its diagonal indicator provides a forbidden commuting matrix. If there are multiple components, their weights can be chosen, by adjusting loops while preserving every nonzero position, so two groups share eigenvalue 1. Right and left eigenvectors then produce a nonzero commuting matrix between those groups, contradicting the requirement.

The matching construction puts vertices along a path, includes both directions between neighbors, and adds one loop at an endpoint. This prior construction has 2n−1 arcs and is strongly connected. The note proves that every permitted weighting works. The support condition first zeros F’s entries immediately beside the diagonal and at the loop. Commutation equations propagate vanishing along successive odd-distance diagonals, using the path boundary. The endpoint loop then forces the remaining first-column entries to vanish. Finally, repeatedly applying A to the first coordinate vector generates a basis: each successive new coordinate contains a product of nonzero path weights. Since F commutes with A and kills that initial vector, it kills the entire basis. For one vertex, a single nonzero loop suffices.

The authors consequently obtain the same minimum for strongly connected supports, whether requiring or merely allowing nSSP, and extend an earlier obstruction for tree supports to all weakly connected supports. Equality identifies the n-dimensional commuting space with the balanced-flow space, but the note does not classify all equality supports or settle general sign-pattern allowability. The authors report Lean formalizations of the central bound, connectivity, endpoint construction, and universal requiring minimum; the component formula and equality identification are written deductions rather than additional named formal statements. Reported exact rational and finite-field computations cover only finite integer-matrix samples. The universal conclusions rest on the proofs, not those samples, and concern every positive order and every permitted real weighting.
